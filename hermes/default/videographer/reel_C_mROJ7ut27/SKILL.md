## Caption
7,376 likes, 112 comments - calebspillyards on September 6, 2024: "Here are some shortcuts to immediately make it sure you get cinematic footage 🎥 ⬇️ 

1.  These settings are an industry standard.  You want your frame rate at 23.976 or 24 to make it easy.  You want your shutter to double that at 1/48 or 1/50 for most non cinema cameras.  This gives you a natural motion blur. An aperture at 2.8 will give you a cinematic depth of field and still be sharp with most lenses.  ISO at 800 for shooting lot (or whatever your cameras native iso is, some are 640.  I have my camera at these settings 95 percent of the time and use an ND filter to change exposure.

2.  This is a big ones.  Lens compression is what our eyes are used to seeing in movies.  Shooting at 35mm and above give you a nice compressed frame that automatically feels more cinematic.  That’s not to say you can never go wider for establishing shots or to purposely extend your frame.  But tight lenses help a cinematic look.

3.  Camera movement is also a huge factor when it comes to making things look cinematic.  Adding a slow push or pull, tracking with your subject or using a gimbal to add dynamic camera moves goes a long way to making things look “expensive”

Bonus:  If you’re shooting yourself like I am a lot, most of your shots will be static.  That’s okay.  Static shots can look great too but this is really when you want to focus on a strong interesting composition, and since you can’t have movement from the camera, be sure there’s movement in your frame.
